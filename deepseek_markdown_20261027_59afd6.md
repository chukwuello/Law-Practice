# Legal Practice Pleading

Generate structured, jurisdiction-aware legal pleadings and court documents using a disciplined drafting methodology. Designed for attorneys, paralegals, and law students who require a reliable first draft that must be reviewed and finalized by a licensed legal professional.

---

## Description

This skill assists with drafting formal legal documents such as complaints, answers, motions, briefs, and responses. It emphasizes procedural compliance, factual clarity, accurate citation, and ethical safeguards. It does **not** provide legal advice, create an attorney–client relationship, or replace independent professional judgment.

**Critical distinction:** Pleadings contain **material facts** only—never the evidence by which those facts are to be proved. This skill enforces that principle throughout.

## Core Principle: Facts vs. Evidence

> **Facts are pleaded; evidence is proved.**  
> Pleadings contain *material facts* only—never the evidence by which they are to be proved.

### What Goes in a Pleading
- Material facts constituting the cause of action or defence.
- Dates, amounts, names, and events.
- The reliefs sought.

### What Does NOT Go in a Pleading
- The evidence by which facts will be proved (e.g., receipts, witness statements, documents).
- Argumentative statements or legal conclusions.
- Hearsay or commentary.

### Examples

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| "The Claimant paid ₦500,000 to the Defendant as evidenced by the receipt attached hereto." | "The Claimant paid ₦500,000 to the Defendant on 1st January 2024." |
| "The Defendant admitted liability in a WhatsApp message dated 2nd February." | "On 2nd February 2024, the Defendant admitted liability." |
| "The Claimant graduated from GSS Garki in 2005, as shown in the certificate." | "The Claimant graduated from GSS Garki in 2005." |

### Key Authorities
- **Aghadiuno v. Onubogu (1998) 5 NWLR (Pt. 548) 16**: Averments in pleadings are not evidence.
- **Ijomah v. Molokwu (2025) 21 NWLR (Pt. 2023) 145**: Evidence at variance with pleadings is inadmissible and goes to no issue.
- **FCT High Court Civil Procedure Rules, Order 15 Rule 2**: Every pleading shall contain a statement in summary form of the material facts on which the party pleading relies, **but not the evidence** by which they are to be proved.

## When to Use

- Drafting initial pleadings (complaint, petition, answer)
- Preparing motions and responses
- Drafting legal memoranda or briefs (with adaptation)
- Organizing facts and legal arguments into a structured document
- Creating a skeleton for attorney review and refinement

## Inputs Required

To produce a useful draft, gather as much of the following as possible:

- **Jurisdiction and court** (e.g., Superior Court of California, County of Los Angeles)
- **Case number** (if assigned)
- **Parties** (full legal names)
- **Document type** (e.g., Complaint for Breach of Contract)
- **Chronological facts** with dates, amounts, and key events
- **Legal claims or defenses** to be asserted
- **Applicable statutes, regulations, and case law**
- **Relief sought** (damages, injunction, specific performance, etc.)
- **Local formatting rules** (font, margins, spacing, page limits)
- **Filing and service requirements**
- **Deadlines and scheduling considerations**

If information is missing, insert clear placeholders such as `[DATE]`, `[AMOUNT]`, or `[CITE]`.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Case Intake and Scope**
   - Confirm the document type, jurisdiction, and purpose.
   - Identify missing facts or authorities.
   - Clarify whether the document is a first draft, outline, or final polish.

2. **Jurisdiction and Procedural Check**
   - Confirm the correct court, venue, and procedural rules.
   - Check whether any special forms, certificates, or cover sheets are required.
   - Note filing deadlines and service requirements.

3. **Legal Research and Authority Validation**
   - Identify controlling statutes, regulations, and case law.
   - Verify that each authority is still good law.
   - Use pinpoint citations where possible.
   - Never fabricate or guess citations.

4. **Fact Organization**
   - Arrange facts chronologically.
   - Separate undisputed facts from disputed facts.
   - Identify which facts support each legal element.
   - **Do not include evidence** (documents, testimony, receipts, etc.).

5. **Outline and Structure**
   - Build the document using the standard pleading structure below.
   - Ensure each claim or defense includes all required elements.
   - Map facts to elements and authorities to arguments.

6. **Draft with Precision**
   - Use formal legal language and plain, unambiguous sentences.
   - Number paragraphs consistently.
   - State one fact or point per paragraph where practical.
   - **Plead facts only, not evidence.**
   - Avoid emotive, argumentative, or conclusory language unless appropriate.
   - Define terms on first use.

7. **Citation and Quotation Verification**
   - Confirm every citation exists and is cited correctly.
   - Check quotations against the original source.
   - Include subsequent history if relevant.
   - Follow Bluebook or jurisdiction-specific citation rules.

8. **Compliance and Formatting Check**
   - Apply local rules for font, margins, spacing, and page limits.
   - Include all required sections, certificates, and attachments.
   - Confirm the caption matches the court’s requirements.

9. **Review Checklist**
   - Run through the quality checklist below.
   - Mark any unresolved issues clearly for attorney review.

10. **Final Output**
    - Provide a clean draft in Markdown or plain text.
    - Include a short list of missing information and verification warnings.
    - Remind the user that attorney review is required before filing.
